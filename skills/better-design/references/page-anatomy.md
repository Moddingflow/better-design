# Page anatomy: build the page from the visitor's job

Read for any landing, marketing, event, shop, or portfolio page. The visitor's job decides
where the action lives, which sections the category actually has, and how the page ends. The
direction dresses this anatomy; the drawn shape orders it; neither replaces it.

The job-based anatomy, hero postures, and page budgets are adapted from Hallmark (MIT,
github.com/Nutlope/hallmark), which derived them from real independent sites. Our wording
and selection.

## Jobs

Every job: the nav carries the job's own verb (Book, Tickets, Shop, Listen, Start free), the
first screen shows the thing itself, and proof comes in the category's own form, never a
generic band of numbers. When the brief gives none of a section's content, build it as a
clearly marked sample the user replaces (sample dishes, sample issue titles); a sample never
states a price, date, name, count, or review as fact.

| Job | Where the action lives | The category's own sections | How it ends |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| subscribe (newsletter, podcast, waitlist) | in the first screen, often as the first screen: name, one line, the field | a sample issue set as it arrives, the archive as an index, who writes it | the archive, or a second field under it; the one job whose close may be a form |
| book (restaurant, class, clinic, studio) | booking times or a picker in the nav and first screen, repeated beside the menu/classes | the room or food as a photograph, the menu or timetable, how to find it, hours and address | hours, address, and the booking again |
| visit (shop, gallery, venue) | today's hours and address in the nav or first screen | what is on now, what is coming as dated rows, the shelves or rooms, getting there | visit information and the address |
| attend (event, festival, race) | tickets in the nav from the first screen; date and place are the headline | lineup set large, the days as a schedule, ticket tiers, venue and travel, questions | tickets again, or the questions |
| buy (product, small shop) | the product with its options as the first screen, or a sticky buy bar | the range, the thing in use close up, how it is made, delivery and care as facts | the offer again or the guarantee; an email field belongs in the footer |
| try (app, game, desktop tool) | store or download buttons under the head | named moments shown in real screens, a before and after, platforms | the download beside one last screen |
| sign up (software, API) | start free in nav and first screen, terms in one line; a dev tool may use the install command | the product at work, a code sample or integration, pricing table, questions | the first screen's action again, or the code sample; never a newsletter |
| hire (studio, freelancer) | a plain email or phone in the nav and after the work | the work as an index of projects, one opened as a case, what the studio does | contact details |
| join (club, community) | join or "your first visit" in the first screen | what a session is like, when and where, membership options, first-visit guidance | the first visit or the options |
| listen (musician, label) | the player or record in the first screen | tracks as an index, dates with ticket links, earlier work, a live photograph | the dates or more of the catalogue |

**Stock close:** a closing email sign-up on a page whose job is not subscribe, join, attend,
listen, or visit is the template's ending. End on the job's action or its facts.

## First-screen postures

Pick one from the job and the material, and vary it from build to build. Text beside a
picture (settled, stage) is the most common generated hero: take it only when the picture is
the strongest thing the brief has.

| Posture | Shape |
| --- | --- |
| settled | text and visual share the screen, centred in it |
| stage | text on 5 of 12 columns, a visual on 7 at 60–75% of the screen height; may bleed off the edge |
| banner | half height; the first section shows above the fold (references, docs, utilities) |
| ledge | a tall field above, the head resting at its base |
| corridor | one centred column of 44ch or less, the head larger than usual |
| poster | full-bleed picture or canvas, content on its calmest part |
| counter | the action is the visual: booking times, the field with the latest issue, the buy box |
| index | the list is the visual: the menu, the lineup, the issues, the projects |

## Shapes

A vocabulary for naming what you built, not a menu to pick from first: photo-led folds,
marquee first screen, diptych rows (text and proof in alternating halves with varied
weight), catalogue (a uniform grid of variants), timeline (a day, a season, a job done),
mosaic (an irregular grid of real items), interview (questions as headings), letter
(first person, signed), long document, manifesto, index-first (the page is a list), sticky
story (a pinned head with scrolling proof), workbench tour (real screens as the content),
map or diagram (one spatial model organises the page), number-led (a real figure the user
gave, and what it means).

## Budgets for expressive pages

These hold for landing, event, shop, and portfolio pages; working tools follow their task.

- **First screen at 1280×800:** the headline, one sentence, and the primary action are all
  visible without scrolling. Headline about seven words or fewer, the sentence about twelve.
- **About 80 words per screen.** Fullness comes from scale, pictures, and the product shown,
  never from more words. Six to nine sections including hero and footer (five on a thin
  brief), around 450 visible words in total.
- **One idea per screen, set big.** Section heads around 40px or more at 1440 wide on
  expressive pages (long documents and letters excepted). Every section has mass: a visual,
  a set of three or more real items, a table, or a statement at section size; a head and a
  paragraph alone is unfinished.
- **Rhythm:** section gaps vary (not one value everywhere) and stay generous; the space above
  a heading is at least twice the space below it; adjacent sections differ in structure.
- **Container:** capped and centred; at 1920 the gutters grow, the measure and grids do not.
- **Pictures:** at most about a third of the page below the hero, never two photo-led
  sections in a row; the material's own type, figures, and product carry the rest.
- **Invariants:** one primary button recipe, one container width, one radius per nesting
  level, never more centred heading groups than left-aligned ones.
